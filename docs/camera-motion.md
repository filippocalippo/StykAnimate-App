# Camera motion and layer parallax

Select **Camera → Motion** to add a move. With no moves, the tab opens on a gallery that describes each preset: Follow, Shake, Handheld, Wobble, Impact punch and Zoom pulse; afterwards **Add** reopens it above the list. A selected move shows its timing (start, length, ease in and out) and a few sliders with plain names (Amount, Intensity, Rotation, Speed, Zoom, Settle); each slider covers the useful range and its typed value reaches the full saved range. Intensity sets Move X and Y together. **Fine tune** holds the exact per-axis values, noise, seed and phase. Drag numeric labels horizontally to adjust values; Shift gives fine control and Alt gives extra-fine control. Click a label to type instead. Numeric edits preview immediately; releasing a drag or pressing Enter commits one undo step, and Escape restores the previous value. Timing and fades stay in whole frames. Seed uses typing or Randomize. Preview plays the selected clip's range; stopping restores the normal transport range.

## Follow

**Follow** keeps figures in frame. Right-click figures and choose **Follow with camera** (or use the command palette), or pick Follow in the gallery with figures selected: the move runs from the playhead to the end of the animation. The card lists the followed figures; **Follow selected figures** adds more, and with several figures each has a weight for how much it pulls the shot. **Amount** blends between the authored camera and the follow. **Smoothness** is how many frames the camera takes to catch up (0 locks on), **Dead zone** is the share of the frame the figures can cross before the camera reacts, **Look ahead** leads the shot toward where they are going, and **Direction** follows both axes, across or up/down. **Auto zoom** zooms in or out to keep every followed figure in view with a **Margin**, up to a **Zoom limit** either way. **Offset X/Y** places the figures away from the centre, and **Keep current framing** sets it from where they are in the shot now. Overlapping follows share one shot: their weights blend, so fading one out while another fades in crosses from one figure to the other.

Follow moves the camera itself. It plays on top of the authored camera keys, before the other presets' impulse, and parallax layers respond to it like authored camera travel, without baking. **Bake to keys** keeps it as travel: only the other presets become the baked parallax impulse.

`camera-follow.mjs` samples the followed figures from a projection that holds only those figures, their join parents and the camera (other figures are never copied): per frame the weighted centre of their artwork, their union bounds and the authored camera. A forward pass shapes these samples in the authored camera's output pixels: dead zone with hysteresis, look ahead, a critically damped spring for smoothness, and the framing zoom. Evaluation is therefore deterministic from timeline time, with no playback state. Samples and shaped paths extend lazily to the frame being viewed, played or exported. After an edit, layer-sheet tracks are compared key by key and samples are taken again only from the key before the first change; shaping resumes from checkpoints every 32 frames. Parameter edits reuse the samples. Definitions, animation channels, layer assignments and non-sheet documents invalidate the whole path. A follow clip lasts at most 100,000 frames and follows 1–16 figures. In a 1,500-figure scene, sampling costs about 0.16 ms per frame on the development laptop, so the first view of frame 450 took about 70 ms and an edit that resampled 52 frames about 10 ms; playback and export sample in their workers.

The live graph separates **Move** (X/Y in pixels), **Roll** (degrees), and **Zoom** (percent). It plots the selected clip's actual sampled output, with a zero line, frame axis, shaded fades and a playhead. Hover to read a frame's values. Editing movement, roll or zoom selects its graph view; changing a value or fade updates the graph before committing. Moving or resizing a timeline clip also previews its frame range here before release. The vertical scale stays steady when lowering amplitude and expands when needed; **Fit curve** resets it to the current range. Playback and seeking move the cursor without resampling the curve.

Advanced timeline clips appear in one **Motion** row immediately below Camera, with the number of clips beside its name. In start order each clip takes the first sub-lane that is free at its start, so clips that never play together share a sub-lane and the row grows only with the motions playing at once: one lane is an ordinary row, and each further overlapping motion adds a 14 px sub-lane (26 px on touch screens). Clips are tinted by type (Follow blue, Shake orange, Impact punch rose, Handheld teal, Wobble mint, Zoom pulse violet; gold stays the camera's own colour) and the Motion tab uses the same colours for their icons. A clip shows its name when it fits; hovering any clip shows its name and frames, and while a clip is dragged the tip shows the frames it will land on. A muted clip is faded and hatched. Click a clip to select it; an empty stretch clears the motion selection, and the row's name selects the clip at the playhead. The selected clip's trim edges also reach a few pixels into the gaps around its sub-lane. Moving a clip over another moves it to a free sub-lane on release. Use Camera's disclosure to collapse or expand the Motion row; collapsed, Camera keeps a thin ribbon under its keys in the motions' colours, and pressing the ribbon opens the row with that motion selected. Collapsing changes only the timeline view, clears a hidden motion selection, and survives edits and undo. Selecting a motion through the timeline reveals its lanes. Drag a clip's body to move, an edge to resize, or Alt-drag to duplicate. Arrow keys move the selected clip one frame, Shift-arrow moves ten; Ctrl/Cmd+D duplicates and Delete removes it. Copy, cut and paste use the shared StykAnimate clipboard. The row's eye mutes or enables every clip in one undo step; a clip's menu mutes it alone. Camera locking protects motion edits too. Clip timing uses absolute document frames and is independent of figure exposure keys; editing figure exposures does not retime an effect.

Select a figure layer and enable **Parallax**. Choose Distant, Background, Normal, or Foreground, or drag the X/Y labels or type percentages. Linked axes preview together; unlink them for independent responses. The reference button uses the authored camera position at the current frame. The collapsed Preview's Pan slider previews the result without creating camera keys.

| Response | Camera translation |
| --- | --- |
| 0% | No apparent translation from camera travel |
| 100% | Normal world movement |
| Below 100% | Slower apparent movement, useful for distant layers |
| Above 100% | Faster apparent movement, useful for foreground layers |

Parallax affects translation; camera zoom and roll still apply to every layer. Joined components use their root figure's layer response so attachments stay connected. Additive camera motion affects the complete composed shot; shaking does not pull depth layers apart. The reference preserves placement when enabling the effect at that camera position. Editing the world canvas keeps authored figure positions and camera handles; the output monitor, playback and exports show the composed shot.

**Bake to keys** converts all frames or the selected clip's range into held camera keys. It samples all enabled effects overlapping that range and mutes their baked portions. Adjacent curve spans are sampled as guards where needed to preserve every unedited integer output frame; adjoining Auto spans are included because their tangents are shared. The operation is one undo step. Baking is bounded to 100,000 samples including guards, and cancels if the document changes during preparation. Baking creates output-frame samples, so fractional-time interpolation may change. Muted effects remain editable; explicitly enabling a fully baked effect adds it again.

## Saved data and evaluation

- `_styk.cameraMotion` is a versioned immutable table of up to 256 clips. Each clip has a stable ID, preset type, start, duration, enabled flag, numeric parameters and optional baked ranges. Parameters are validated per type. Follow clips add `targets` (1–16 `{uid, weight}` with weights 0–1) and their own parameters (`strength`, `followX`, `followY`, `offsetX`, `offsetY`, `lag`, `deadZone`, `lookAhead`, `zoomFit`, `padding`, `maxZoom`, `fadeIn`, `fadeOut`); older versions of the app cannot open projects that use them. Times are zero-based frame positions internally; the inspector displays the first frame as 1.
- `_styk.layers.byId[id].parallax` stores `enabled`, `linked`, axis responses from 0 to 4, and a finite reference position. Absence means normal movement. Existing files require no migration.
- `camera-motion.mjs` evaluates follow travel (`cameraTravelAt`, which parallax reads) and then the seeded smooth noise and analytical motion of the other presets from timeline time. There is no accumulated random or playback state; seeking, reverse seeking, playback and export share the same sample. Translation and roll add; zoom contributions combine in logarithmic space with a finite bound.
- Authored camera poses remain unchanged. Evaluated frames carry a transient `_cameraMotionTick`; pose snapshots omit it. `effectiveCamera` composes the output camera once. Prepared packets store that resolved camera without the transient clock, preventing double application.
- `layer-parallax.mjs` computes `(1 − response) × (authored camera travel − reference)` per layer. Joined-root resolution uses path compression. Baked camera poses retain `_parallaxMotionOffset` to distinguish baked shake from depth-driving travel; the same camera easing interpolates that offset.
- Motion commands retain only their small tables and any required exposure extension. Layer responses use existing layer commands. Both use the existing revision, undo, saving and worker invalidation paths; original figure definitions and artwork remain shared.
- The inspector graph owns a retained DOM subtree. Parameter changes are coalesced to one chart update per display frame; a single selected-clip cache holds at most 2,049 samples per channel. The graph uses the runtime motion sampler, and cursor updates reuse the sampled geometry. Live timeline timing edits refresh motion row references without rebuilding unrelated key descriptors. Collapse state is local to the mounted timeline and resets for a new document.

## Rendering, workers and exports

GPU rendering updates the existing object transform buffer; parallax creates no alternate meshes or shaders. Canvas rendering translates object groups. Image decode/culling bounds account for those translations. The same projection is used by the monitor, scene effects, prepared GPU/Canvas playback, SVG and direct/sequential/tiled raster export. Movieclip contents inherit their outer instance's layer projection.

Explicit baking uses one short-lived worker. Its projection includes native camera poses, camera channels, the camera exposure track and referenced camera pose sources; figure geometry, unrelated pose sources, audio and media are excluded. Acknowledged 64 KiB chunks bound transport. The worker samples cooperatively and returns one bounded transferable numeric buffer; the editor publishes the prepared camera keys atomically. This final publication is proportional to the number of camera keys generated.

STYK keeps clips and layer responses editable. Native PIV materialization samples their appearance in the export worker and removes procedural metadata. Export conversion never rewrites the open project. Existing export frame/object limits still apply.

## Verification

Run `node --test tests/camera-motion.test.mjs tests/camera-motion-graph.test.mjs`, `node tests/camera-motion.browser.mjs`, `node tests/camera-motion-scrub.browser.mjs`, `node tests/camera-motion-graph.browser.mjs`. Browser tests use the local server from `npm run serve` (`STYK_URL` overrides its address). They exercise live curves and timeline resizing, collapse/expand, label scrubbing, modifiers, cancellation, locks/timing, worker baking, undo, actual binary STYK/PIV round trips, and pixel comparisons across rendering paths, with and without scene effects and movieclips.
